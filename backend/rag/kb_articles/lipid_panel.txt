Lipid Panel (Cholesterol)

A lipid panel measures total cholesterol, LDL ("bad" cholesterol), HDL ("good" cholesterol), and triglycerides. These fats circulate in the blood and are one of the main things doctors look at when estimating heart disease and stroke risk.

High LDL cholesterol is linked to buildup of fatty deposits in blood vessels over time, which can narrow arteries and raise the risk of heart attack or stroke. Mild elevation is often addressed first with diet and exercise; larger elevations, especially with other risk factors, may need medication.

Low HDL cholesterol is also a risk factor, since HDL normally helps remove excess cholesterol from the bloodstream. Higher HDL is generally considered protective.

High triglycerides are often linked to diet, excess weight, alcohol use, or poorly controlled blood sugar, and very high levels can also raise the risk of pancreas inflammation.

A single mildly abnormal lipid result is common and not usually an emergency on its own, but a pattern of abnormal values, or very high numbers, is worth a doctor's review to assess overall cardiovascular risk.
