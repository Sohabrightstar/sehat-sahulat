Liver Function Tests (ALT, AST, Bilirubin)

ALT and AST are enzymes found mainly inside liver cells. When liver cells are irritated or damaged, these enzymes leak into the blood, so higher-than-normal levels usually signal some degree of liver stress or injury rather than a specific disease by themselves.

Common causes of mildly elevated ALT/AST include fatty liver (often linked to weight, alcohol use, or diabetes), certain medications, or a recent viral infection. Very high elevations can indicate more acute liver injury and generally need faster follow-up.

Bilirubin is a byproduct of the normal breakdown of red blood cells that the liver processes and removes. High bilirubin can cause yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice) and can point to liver problems, bile duct blockage, or faster-than-normal red blood cell breakdown.

Because these markers can be affected by many everyday factors (a heavy meal, certain supplements, recent alcohol use, strenuous exercise), a single mildly abnormal result is often repeated or followed up rather than acted on immediately, but a doctor should interpret the pattern together with symptoms.
