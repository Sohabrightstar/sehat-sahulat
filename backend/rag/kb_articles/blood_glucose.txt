Blood Glucose and HbA1c

Fasting blood glucose measures the amount of sugar in the blood after not eating for several hours, and is one of the main tests used to screen for diabetes and prediabetes.

A fasting result that is mildly above the normal range may indicate prediabetes, meaning blood sugar regulation is starting to be impaired but has not reached diabetic levels. This is usually manageable through diet, weight management, and exercise, and is worth discussing with a doctor to catch it early.

A fasting result well above the normal range, especially alongside symptoms like excessive thirst, frequent urination, or unexplained weight loss, is more consistent with diabetes and needs prompt medical follow-up.

Low blood glucose (hypoglycemia) can cause shakiness, sweating, confusion, or fainting, and can be dangerous if severe, particularly in people already being treated for diabetes.

HbA1c is a related test that reflects average blood sugar over the past two to three months rather than a single moment, and is often used alongside fasting glucose to monitor long-term control.
