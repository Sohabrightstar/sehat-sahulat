Electrolytes (Sodium, Potassium)

Sodium and potassium are minerals that help control fluid balance, nerve signaling, and muscle function, including the heartbeat.

Sodium that is too low or too high is usually related to fluid balance — for example, heavy sweating, vomiting, diarrhea, certain medications, or drinking unusually large amounts of water can shift sodium levels. Mild shifts often cause few symptoms, while larger shifts can cause confusion, headache, or weakness and need prompt attention.

Potassium levels are especially important because both very high and very low potassium can affect heart rhythm. Causes of abnormal potassium include certain medications (particularly some blood pressure drugs and diuretics), kidney problems, and significant vomiting or diarrhea.

Because electrolyte abnormalities can affect the heart and nervous system, values that are significantly outside the normal range are treated with more urgency than most other routine lab findings, even if the patient feels only mildly unwell.
