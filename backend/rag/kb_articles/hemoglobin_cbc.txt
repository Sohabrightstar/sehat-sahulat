Hemoglobin and the Complete Blood Count (CBC)

Hemoglobin is the protein inside red blood cells that carries oxygen from the lungs to the rest of the body. A CBC test reports hemoglobin along with related measures like red blood cell count, white blood cell count, and platelet count.

Low hemoglobin (anemia) commonly causes tiredness, pale skin, shortness of breath, and dizziness. Common causes include iron deficiency, vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, chronic blood loss, or long-term illness. Mild anemia is often managed with diet changes or supplements, while more significant drops usually need a doctor to find the underlying cause.

High hemoglobin can occur with dehydration, smoking, living at high altitude, or certain lung and heart conditions that reduce oxygen supply, causing the body to produce more red blood cells.

White blood cells are part of the immune system. A high white blood cell count often points to infection or inflammation somewhere in the body; a low count can mean reduced ability to fight infection and may need further tests depending on the degree of the drop.

Platelets help blood clot. Low platelet counts increase bruising and bleeding risk; high platelet counts are sometimes seen with inflammation or after blood loss, but can also need follow-up if persistent.
